A mid-task goal change can invalidate an agent's next action while preserving work already completed. We compare three ways to respond to such changes in InterventionBench AppWorld tasks: native continuation, full replanning, and local editing of a numbered plan. The three methods resume from identical model-generated prefixes and simulator checkpoints taken before a confirmed withdrawn write. A fourth method receives the complete revised goal from the initial state. Across \StudyCases{} independent families, strict success was \StrictA/\StudyCases{} for native continuation, \StrictB/\StudyCases{} for full replanning, and \StrictC/\StudyCases{} for local revision. The paired local-minus-full difference was \DeltaCB{} percentage points, with a 95\% bootstrap interval of [\LBoundCB, \UBoundCB]. Local revision's two gains came from swaps. Native continuation had the highest observed strict count. Final-state checks passed on several trajectories that still committed a withdrawn effect, illustrating the role of trace evidence. These results describe one model on tasks where an observable intervention boundary could be reached.
