\subsection{Strict success and plan-update strategy}
All \ScoredBranches{} scheduled branches produced terminal scores. One transient network error on a full-replanning branch required a retry from its original checkpoint. Its failed attempt is preserved in the logs. The strict-success comparison uses the complete frozen cohort. All three matching branches for every family passed the checkpoint and visible-history checks.

Native continuation had the highest observed strict count (\StrictA/\StudyCases), followed by local revision (\StrictC/\StudyCases) and full replanning (\StrictB/\StudyCases). Full replanning gained no strict successes and lost \LossesBA{} cases relative to native continuation, a paired difference of \DeltaBA{} percentage points. Local revision gained \GainsCB{} cases and lost none relative to full replanning, a difference of \DeltaCB{} points. The interval runs from \LBoundCB{} to \UBoundCB{} percentage points. With two discordant pairs and both in the same direction, the empirical bootstrap changes how often the two gains are sampled. It cannot establish an advantage on unseen families. The local-minus-native result was \DeltaCA{} points with an interval from \LBoundCA{} to \UBoundCA{}. Table~\ref{tab:paired} reports the matched counts.

Both local gains relative to full replanning occurred among the 26 swaps. The six partial drops yielded four strict successes for every continuation, with the same four families passing. These six cases provide little information about how the strategies would differ on other kinds of partial drops. Table~\ref{tab:strata} gives the stratum-specific denominators.

\subsection{Trace evidence and the standalone comparison}
Of the revised final passes, \MissA/\MissDenA{} for A, \MissB/\MissDenB{} for B, and \MissC/\MissDenC{} for C coexisted with at least one committed withdrawn effect. All three violation denominators were \StudyCases. The final-state and strict columns therefore tell different stories. A and C had the same number of revised final passes, but A had one fewer final pass with a violation. Some failed final states contained violations too.

D passed the revised final checks on \StandaloneSuccess/\StandaloneCases{} families, including \DOnly{} families on which all three continuations failed strict success. On \MatchedOnly{} other families, D failed and at least one continuation succeeded strictly. Table~\ref{tab:standalone} compares final success on the same families. The initial world in D changes which work remains and sometimes how the agent interprets the task.

The frozen retention column had no separately added formal assertions. We therefore conducted a descriptive audit across the six partial drops using existing benchmark checks for retained actions. In each of A, B, C, and D, seven of nine designated conditions passed, one failed, and one was indeterminate. The indeterminate condition is the corrected payment in case 763, for which the evaluator checks a different transaction type from the one directly suggested by the instruction. These are overlapping checks, and the audit leaves frozen scores unchanged.

\subsection{Representative trajectories}
\textbf{Reusing completed work (772).} The original task asked the agent to reorganize meeting files. The prefix moved ten slide files successfully before proposing its first move of a video file. The user limited the work to slides and requested that videos stay in place. Each continuation preserved the ten slide moves and removed an empty directory created during the prefix. A, B, and C passed all four revised assertions. The checkpoint shows why retaining completed effects is important when a later step is withdrawn.

\textbf{A final-state miss (109).} In this case the old task was to total cable bills, and the update replaced it with sending a short email to the simulated account holder. The prefix had already read billing files and was about to submit its answer. Every branch sent the requested email. B and C also committed a nonempty old-task answer through the supervisor completion endpoint after the email. The revised evaluator passed all three continuations, but the state transition detected in B and C made both fail strict success. A passed strict success after sending the email and stopping with text.

\textbf{Local and full replanning diverged (316).} The user changed a request for an Amazon spending total into a request to store the total in a note. The prefix had already gathered the order history. C deleted finished steps, replaced the old answer step, and created the note. A and B created the note and also submitted a nonempty answer. The revised evaluator additionally prohibited all changes to the supervisor task record. D created the note and then issued a completion call without an answer, which still failed that assertion. This design of the official check cautions against attributing the entire C-minus-B difference to the quality of local edits.

\textbf{Instruction and evaluator differ (763).} The intervention asked the agent to skip denying an existing payment request and send a corrected amount. All three continuations created a payment transaction. The evaluator expected a new payment request. D issued that request and passed. This difference illustrates how the adjudicated standalone difficulty can reflect the scoring program's interpretation of a revised instruction.

\subsection{Execution and costs}
The mean number of received model responses per formal branch was 4.78 for A, 5.31 for B, 5.28 for C, and 11.00 for D. These averages include planning where applicable. C used the fallback on one branch, while B used it on none. Successful repeated API requests appeared three times in A, twice in B, and once in C. None of those repeats changed the monitored records. A smaller bill can also reflect early termination, cache usage, or differences in how much of a task the branch completed. These totals give resource context without identifying the more efficient strategy at matched quality.

The project recorded \TotalReceipts{} provider receipts. The peak-price bound on accounted spend was \$\PeakCost, and receipt times give an off-peak estimate of \$\ScheduledCost{}. An additional \$\UnresolvedCost{} was reserved for two requests with ambiguous transport outcomes. Formal prefix screening accounted for \$\PrefixCost{} at peak prices, of which \$\SharedPrefixCost{} generated the admitted checkpoints and \$\RejectedPrefixCost{} went to unadmitted attempts. D accounted for \$\StandaloneCost{} in addition to the A/B/C suffixes. Prices and token counts are reported separately from the provider's invoice.
